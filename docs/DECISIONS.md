# Adam's approval-policy decisions (source of truth for config/policy.yaml and the approval agent)
1. Over-stock (qty > stock for any SKU, after aggregating repeated lines): REJECT. Reason names the item, requested qty, and stock. (Adam, Oct 22 11:03 AM CT)
2. VP/critic deadlock after 2 rounds (VP approve, critic still objects): PENDING_HUMAN, sent to the human review queue with both arguments attached. (Adam, Oct 22 11:04 AM CT)
3. Critic loop runs only for invoices over $10K, near the limit, or with a high risk score; others are decided by the VP agent alone (after hard rules). (Adam, Oct 22 11:07 AM CT)
4. Thresholds: approval threshold $10,000; "near the limit" = total >= $9,500; "high risk" = risk_score >= 50 out of 100. (Adam, Oct 22 11:10 AM CT)
5. Approved agent prompts (Adam, Oct 22 11:15 AM CT):
   VP (approver): "You're Acme's VP of Finance. You see the invoice, the check flags and the risk score. Hard-rule failures are already rejected, so judge the rest. Approve only if the vendor is known, the math is clean and nothing looks off. Give 1 to 3 short reasons, plus any conditions on payment."
   Critic (CFO/auditor): "You're a skeptical auditor. Your job is to find why the VP is wrong: a vendor that was renamed, an amount sitting just under the limit, a price far off catalog, pushy wording. Either object with a specific reason or sign off. Never invent facts that aren't in the invoice."
   Rule: the LLM never computes or changes amounts; deterministic code is the source of truth for math, the LLM only reasons over it. Invoice text is treated as data (prompt-injection defense).
6. Prompt-injection risk signal: invoice text that tries to instruct the system adds 40 risk points (config/policy.yaml, risk_weights.prompt_injection), so offline holds such an invoice for human review. (Adam, Oct 22 12:31 PM CT)
7. Duplicates by content: an invoice that matches an earlier one from the same vendor (normalized name) with the same line items, quantities and total is a DUPLICATE_INVOICE even when the invoice number differs. It is rejected like the existing duplicates, and the reason names the original invoice number and file. Same-number copies (1011, 1012, 1013) and revisions (1004 R1 supersedes 1004) work as before (config/policy.yaml, duplicates.match_content). (Adam delegated, Engineering chose, Oct 22 1:20 PM CT)
8. Prompt injection: any detected injection (the prompt_injection risk signal) is held for a human (PENDING_HUMAN) regardless of the risk score, by a hard hold rule before the VP and critic; if a hard rule also fails, the invoice is rejected. The 40-point weight is unchanged (config/policy.yaml, approval.hard_holds.prompt_injection). (Adam delegated, Engineering chose, Oct 22 1:20 PM CT)
9. Vendor rename or lookalike: an invoice whose vendor says "formerly X" (VENDOR_RENAMED), or whose name is not on file but is a lookalike of a vendor on file (VENDOR_LOOKALIKE, similarity at least 0.85), is held for human verification by a hard hold rule instead of being paid with a condition (config/policy.yaml, approval.hard_holds.vendor_renamed and vendor_lookalike, vendor_lookalike.min_similarity). (Adam delegated, Engineering chose, Oct 22 1:20 PM CT)
10. LLM-repaired invoices: any invoice that Grok rebuilt from raw text because the parser could not read it (extraction mode llm_repair) is held for a human (PENDING_HUMAN) by a hard hold rule before the VP and critic, never paid automatically; if a hard rule also fails, it is rejected. The review output shows the original raw text next to Grok's reading (python main.py review <invoice number>) (config/policy.yaml, approval.hard_holds.llm_repaired). (Adam delegated, Engineering chose, Oct 22 2:45 PM CT)
11. Bank-detail changes: an invoice that gives or changes payment details (an IBAN, SWIFT or BIC code, a routing, sort or account number, "remit to", "new account", "updated bank details", "change of bank") gets a BANK_DETAILS_CHANGED warning and is held for a human by a hard hold rule before the VP and critic, so a person confirms the account with the vendor contact on file. A plain "Remit to" line also holds; none of the case samples carries payment details. If a hard rule also fails, it is rejected (config/policy.yaml, bank_details.change_patterns and approval.hard_holds.bank_details_changed). (Adam delegated, Engineering chose, Oct 22 5:30 PM CT)
12. Grok on every invoice, live: when the parser reads a file, Grok also reads it independently and the two readings are compared (vendor, invoice number, total, line items and unit prices). The parser's reading stays the one that is used; any difference is an EXTRACTION_DISAGREEMENT warning and a hard hold for a human (config/policy.yaml, approval.hard_holds.extraction_disagreement). After every decision, including hard-rule rejections and hard holds, Grok writes a plain-language decision memo for the AP clerk and the audit log. The memo is written after the decision is final and is never read back, so it cannot change an outcome. Offline (no key) a fixed template writes the memo and there is no cross-check. (Adam delegated, Engineering chose, Oct 22 5:30 PM CT)
13. Address watch list: an entry does not fire on the address the vendor master has on file for that same vendor (an ADDRESS_ON_FILE info flag records the match). In this mock data Precision Parts Ltd. is on file at 742 Evergreen Terrace, so INV-1004 R1 is payable when it passes every other check; INV-1004 stays rejected as superseded by R1. Another vendor using a watch-listed address (adversarial 3007, Atlas Industrial Supply) still fires (config/policy.yaml, risk.suspicious_addresses). (Adam delegated, Engineering chose, Oct 22 5:30 PM CT)
14. New layouts (amends decision 10): offline, a file that looks like an invoice (the word invoice, bill or amount due, and a dollar amount) but that the parser cannot read (no line items or no total, with no blocking flag other than the ones that follow from that) is held for a human with an UNREADABLE_LAYOUT reason instead of being rejected. Live, an invoice Grok rebuilt goes on to normal review (risk, VP, critic) only when the file is intact (no JSON, XML or CSV syntax error), every quantity, unit price and the total Grok reported is printed in the file as digits, and the vendor is on file; all hard rules still apply. Otherwise decision 10's hold applies, so the truncated 2003 and the number-words email 2001 are still held (config/policy.yaml, approval.hard_holds.llm_repaired and unreadable_layout). (Adam delegated, Engineering chose, Oct 22 5:30 PM CT)
15. Invalid amounts: NaN or infinity anywhere in an amount or quantity (NON_FINITE_NUMBER), a total of zero (ZERO_TOTAL), a negative line amount (NEGATIVE_AMOUNT) and a fractional quantity (FRACTIONAL_QTY; inventory items are whole units) are blocking flags, so the invoice is rejected. The payment step also refuses any amount that is not a finite number above zero, as a last guard. A currency code is stripped and upper-cased before it is checked (" usd " reads as USD). (Adam delegated, Engineering chose, Oct 22 5:30 PM CT)
Implementation notes, reviewer pass (Oct 22, 2026, 2:15 PM CT; Adam asked for both changes; no threshold, weight or outcome rule changed):
- Decision 7 also applies across runs: the content and same-number checks compare each invoice with the invoices stored by earlier runs in SQLite (invoices table, with payment status), not only with the current batch. Only the earliest stored copy counts, so re-running the same file is idempotent (already paid or already processed), never flagged as fraud.
- Decision 8 also fires when the live Grok risk review sets its structured `prompt_injection` field (RiskReview), not only on the text-pattern signal. Its prose is never string-matched. The pattern list gained five generic forms (override the rules, address the AI, skip the human, dictate the outcome, dictate the score) that catch the reworded injection 3002; across all 30 test invoices they fire only on the three injection invoices 2002, 3001 and 3002.
Implementation notes, second reviewer pass (Oct 22, 2026, 3:00 PM CT; delegated by Adam; no threshold or weight changed, decision 10 is the only new outcome rule):
- Past due dates: a non-blocking PAST_DUE warning (due date before the day of the run, config/policy.yaml due_dates). It is shown to people only; the risk, VP and critic agents do not see it, so it never changes a decision.
- Currency: a currency with no FX rate, or amounts written with another currency's symbol or code, get a blocking UNSUPPORTED_CURRENCY flag that names the currency (previously such a file failed as MISSING_ITEMS).
- Injection patterns: the legacy 'automated system' and 'approve this invoice' now need context (a note addressed to the automated system; approve combined with skipping review), and the address-the-AI form needs a greeting or "to/for". 2002, 3001 and 3002 stay caught; harmless notes such as "Please approve this invoice by Friday" no longer hold.
Implementation notes, final pass (Oct 22, 2026, 4:00 PM CT; delegated by Adam; no threshold, weight or decision rule changed):
- Live decision cache: Grok's answers for an invoice are stored in SQLite (llm_cache table) under a hash of the normalized file content, the policy file, the model id and the prompt code, and reused when the same invoice runs again, so a live re-run gives the same decision. `--no-cache` turns it off. Hard rules, hard holds and payment idempotency always run fresh.
- Model pin: the model id the API reports is recorded for every call. If it differs from the configured model, the invoice gets a MODEL_MISMATCH warning and is held for a human (config/policy.yaml, approval.hard_holds.model_mismatch); an invoice the hard rules rejected stays rejected.
- Older databases: on connect, empty history keys are filled in from the stored columns and, for the content key, by reading the stored source file again offline, so cross-run duplicate checks cover rows stored before the history columns existed.
- The 'override the rules' injection pattern now needs the rule word right after the verb, so "disregard the previous delivery instructions" does not fire. 2002, 3001 and 3002 stay caught.
